<objective>
Retarget the scripts that read the retired tree, repoint the two closed-phase asserts whose fixtures
moved, and assert criterion 6 as a whole: the tree is gone and no in-scope script reads it.

The scan needs care. RESEARCH F-9 measured thirty files holding a repo-root-relative reference to that
path, most of them pointing at paths that have been missing for the whole milestone and are broken
today independently of this phase. A literal repo-wide assert would fail on day one against files
including a closed record that must not be edited. The scope decision is settled: the twelve moved
files, plus the two nvim scripts and the system-monitor installer, which are in `scripts/` and `arch/`,
are currently broken, and are cheap. The rest is backlog.

Two traps inside that set. The Phase 17 assert's hits are quoted grep *patterns*, not reads, and that
file must not be edited. The wrapper's delete guard holds a live-path glob that protects the same
directory from destructive removal; retargeting or deleting it would weaken a shipped safety control.

Phase 17's anchored-pattern technique solves the bare-versus-anchored discrimination problem, but its
regex deliberately treats a repo-root-variable-anchored path as *correct*, because Phase 17 was banning
working-directory-relative paths. This phase bans the path regardless of anchoring, so it needs a
second pattern. Copy the technique and its comment discipline, not the regex.

Purpose: close criterion 6 without editing a closed record and without a ban that passes over nothing.
Output: five retargeted scripts, a backlog record, and assert section 6.
</objective>
